What administrators
ask first.

Pricing is shared in the partnership conversation. Bundle pricing depends on course volume, student volume and length of commitment, and pilot pricing is available on request.

01

Whose transcript do students receive?

Yours. Your school remains the school of record. The Academy delivers coursework and reports grades. You issue the final transcript and diploma.

02

Do we need to run the LMS?

No. The Academy handles Buzz LMS provisioning, support and student accounts. There is no platform contract to negotiate and no sysadmin to hire.

03

What happens to our faculty?

Academy courses extend your catalog. They do not displace in-house teachers. Partners use them for electives, scheduling gaps, credit recovery and specialized upper-school content.

04

How is student data handled?

The Academy signs a standard partner agreement with FERPA-aligned data handling. Student data moves only between your registrar and the advising team. The Academy does not market to your students or families.

05

Can we pilot before committing?

Yes. Pilot partners often start with one or two courses for a small cohort and expand the following year.

06

What about our denominational distinctives?

The Academy is broadly classical Christian. It will review doctrinal alignment with your leadership before anything is signed.

04How a student is formed

Classical education.
Lasting formation.

The Trivium is a two-thousand-year-old tradition of Grammar, Logic and Rhetoric. Each stage builds on the one before it. The order is the point.

The Trivium,
stage by stage.

Choose a stage to see what it trains and how it is taught here.

Acquiring knowledge

Learn the vocabulary, facts and structure of each discipline.

The periodic table in chemistry, scales in music, creeds in theology, names and dates in history. A student at this stage can say what they are studying and find their place within it.

How it is taught Structured reading, recall practice and AI-tutored drills, paced slowly on purpose.

Reasoning and discernment

Argue carefully, reason from first principles, and spot a weak argument, your own included.

A student at this stage can take a claim apart, ask better questions, and change their mind when the evidence warrants.

How it is taught Socratic discussion in the weekly one-on-one advisory, formal argumentation, and writing that has to defend a position.

Communication and wisdom

Say what is true with clarity, beauty and conviction, and with respect for the listener.

By Grade 12 a student should be able to write a paragraph that earns its conclusion and give a speech worth hearing.

How it is taught Essays read and graded by real teachers, oration and persuasion courses, public presentations and senior projects.

Theology and Scripture run through every discipline and are not set apart as an elective. Character formation is part of the academic work.

The Five
Pillars.

Five conditions a student needs in order to grow. Each one is said in the student's own voice.

01

Seen

I am known.

A student here is a particular person, never an enrollment number. A dedicated Advisor knows their story, strengths and struggles, meets them every week, and notices trouble early. Parents hear real observations about growth, beyond grades.

Genesis 16:13

02

Loved

I belong.

Belonging is given and does not have to be earned. Worth does not rise or fall with performance or behavior. Advisors lead with warmth and patience, correction happens inside the relationship, and no student is written off.

1 John 4:19

03

Safe

I can take risks here.

Growth needs risk, and risk needs safety: emotional, relational and intellectual. Mistakes are part of learning. Expectations are clear and feedback is direct and kind. Advisors work in a trauma-informed way and mentor; when a student needs clinical care they refer out and work with the family.

Psalm 46:1

04

Accountable

My choices matter.

Accountability is relational and treats the student as a moral agent, which is a form of respect. Students own their learning, behavior and commitments: meeting deadlines, showing up, coming back after a setback. It is never offered as rejection, and parents are partners in it.

Proverbs 27:17

05

Confident

I can act with courage.

Confidence here means a settled freedom to act with courage, humility and purpose, grounded in knowing who you are and whose you are. Students take on harder work, public speaking, original thinking and leadership, and are sent out into the world.

2 Timothy 1:7

Five documents
to gather.

You may submit before everything is ready and add the rest later in the portal. A decision is made once all required documents are in.

01

Proof of identity

The student's passport, national ID or birth certificate.

02

Proof of residence

A recent utility bill, lease or government letter showing the current address.

03

School transcript

The most recent official transcript from the current or previous school. Transfer credits are accepted, and mid-year transfers are welcome.

04

Character reference

From a community leader outside the family, such as a pastor, coach, teacher or mentor. The student requests it and uploads it.

05

Personal essay

Written by the student, on why they want to attend. There is no required length.

If identity, residence or transcript documents are unavailable, a signed affidavit can stand in for them, and templates are inside the application. The reference and the essay cannot be replaced.

How the form works

It opens in the Academy's student information system.

Start the application

  • Sections in order: privacy, basic information, guardian details, financial information, supporting documents
  • Use "Finish Later" to save. It creates a portal login and emails you instructions
  • Check your status in the portal at any time. The school emails as it reviews
  • Application fee $100, which may be waived for financial circumstances

Motivated students in Grades 9 to 12 who want more than a collection of credits: partner-school students, homeschoolers and independent learners anywhere in the world.
