03Resources and safety

Prevention, preparation,
response.

Every location, activity and lodging is assessed before a group arrives. Trained staff stay with students throughout, and families are kept informed.

In the field.

Staff are trained in first aid, emergency response and risk management for this region: coastal water, rainforest trails and tropical heat.

01

A health form before arrival

A confidential form where families disclose allergies, medications, conditions and dietary needs.

02

Medical kits on site

Each trip carries a kit for common travel illnesses, injuries and allergic reactions.

03

Clinics and hospital

Staff keep contacts and transport plans for the clinics in Manuel Antonio and Quepos and for the regional hospital.

04

Evacuation plans

There is a plan for every stop on the itinerary.

05

Families told promptly

Parents and guardians are notified of any injury, illness, incident needing medical attention, or change of itinerary.

The two-adult rule

No adult is ever alone with a student.

It holds in every student interaction.

  • Every adult staff member, volunteer and contractor who works directly with students passes a background check first
  • A national criminal background check, renewed regularly
  • Professional and personal references are contacted before onboarding
  • Training to recognize, prevent and report suspected abuse or misconduct involving minors

04Code of conduct

How we live
for ten days.

Students and families sign the code during enrollment. Accepted students find the signable version in the student portal.

01

Respect for people

Speak courteously to hosts, staff, drivers and each other. Follow staff instructions the first time. No harassment or demeaning humor. Ask before photographing people, homes or places of worship.

02

Respect for culture and place

Make an effort to speak Spanish. Dress modestly for churches, homes, schools and rural communities. Eat what is served gratefully, and disclose allergies in advance. Leave no trace outdoors, and never handle or feed wildlife.

03

Safety and supervision

Do not leave the group, lodging or activity area without staff permission. Stay with your buddy or small group in public. Keep curfew and stay in your assigned room overnight. Swim, surf, hike or climb only when staff authorize it. Report injuries and unsafe situations at once.

04

What is prohibited

Alcohol, tobacco, vaping, cannabis and non-prescribed substances. Weapons. Romantic or sexual activity, or entering another student's sleeping quarters. Theft, vandalism, lying about whereabouts, and independent travel. These lead to immediate review and possible removal from the program at the family's expense.

05

Technology and communication

Phones stay away during class, meals, worship, service work and conversations with hosts. Use the scheduled daily windows to message or call home. Do not post about others without permission, and do not share host family or lodging locations.

06

Academic honesty

Submit your own observations and writing. Cite the guides, texts and people whose ideas you use. Come to Socratic discussion having done the reading, and argue in good faith.

How we
respond.

Three steps, in order of seriousness.

01

A conversation

Staff begin with a direct, restorative conversation.

02

A formal meeting

Repeated or serious breaches lead to a meeting with the program director and a call home.

03

Removal

Conduct that endangers a student, a host or the program leads to removal at the family's expense, with travel arranged by the program team.

Families are invited to talk the code through with the program before applying.

05Questions

What families and leaders
ask first.

Answers drawn from what the program has published. Where the program has not published an answer, we say so and point you to the team.

About the program

01

What is GP Immersion?

A ten-day, Christ-centered immersion for high school students in Manuel Antonio, Costa Rica, joining the Academy's classical Christian curriculum to full cultural immersion.

02

Who is it for?

High school students, in groups sent by schools, churches or youth organizations. The inquiry form also takes families and other grade levels.

03

How long is it?

Ten days.

04

When does it start?

Inquiries are open for Spring, Summer and Fall 2027, and for 2028 or later.

Safety and health

05

How are students kept safe?

Locations, activities and lodging are assessed before arrival. Trained staff stay with students throughout, under a two-adult rule, and every adult who works with students is background-checked.

06

What if a student is sick or injured?

Each trip carries a medical kit. Staff keep contacts and transport plans for clinics in Manuel Antonio and Quepos and for the regional hospital, and families are notified promptly.

07

Are health forms required?

Yes. A confidential health form covers allergies, medications, conditions and dietary needs before arrival. For vaccination requirements, ask the team.

Formation and daily life

08

What academics are included?

Marine science testing and rainforest ecology in the field, with reading and Socratic discussion drawn from the Academy's curriculum.

09

How does faith shape the experience?

Students worship and grow with the local church, serve alongside a church partner, and live the ten days in Christian community.

10

How often can students contact family?

There are scheduled daily windows to message or call home. Phones stay away during class, meals, worship and service work.

11

Do students need to speak Spanish?

They are asked to make an effort. Culture and Spanish is one of the program's themes.

12

Who travels with the group?

Each group brings one adult chaperone for every ten students, preferably with at least one adult of each gender.
